# DON’T LOOK HUMAN

## AI-Ready Game Design Brief

### Project Status

**Don’t Look Human** is the final game concept I have chosen to develop.

It replaces my earlier crab survival-game idea. This is **not** an endless score-chasing game about avoiding sharks, rocks, or falling obstacles. The new project is a low-poly 3D behavior-stealth game with a more original identity and greater potential for expansion.

---

## One-Sentence Pitch

**Don’t Look Human is a low-poly 3D behavior-stealth game where the player controls a suspiciously intelligent animal and must blend in with ordinary animals, complete secret objectives, and escape before nearby humans realize that something is wrong.**

---

## Main Concept

The player controls an animal living in an environment occupied by normal NPC animals and human characters.

Unlike a traditional stealth game where the player simply hides behind walls, the player hides by **behaving naturally**.

The player may look like an ordinary pigeon, but the way they move can reveal that they are being controlled by a human. Running directly toward an objective, turning too perfectly, repeatedly jumping, staring at humans, or moving with obvious purpose can attract attention.

To survive, the player must observe nearby animals and imitate their behavior while secretly completing objectives.

The player is always balancing two goals:

1. Make progress toward the objective.
2. Avoid looking intelligent or player-controlled.

The central design rule is:

> **Move like an animal, not like a gamer.**

---

## Genre

* Behavior-stealth
* Animal simulation
* Light comedy
* Objective-based adventure
* Low-poly 3D indie game

The game should feel playful, strange, tense, and funny rather than violent or overly serious.

---

## Player Fantasy

The player should feel like they are secretly much smarter than everyone around them while pretending to be a harmless animal.

The fun comes from situations such as:

* Acting innocent while approaching something valuable.
* Copying the behavior of NPC animals.
* Freezing or walking away when a human becomes suspicious.
* Creating distractions without revealing intelligence.
* Completing an objective while appearing to behave randomly.
* Escaping just before a ranger figures out what is happening.

---

## First Playable Animal

The first playable animal will be a **pigeon**.

The pigeon works well because:

* Pigeons naturally gather in public areas.
* They frequently walk, peck, fly short distances, and approach food.
* Strange pigeon behavior can be funny.
* Groups of NPC pigeons provide opportunities for blending in.
* A pigeon can access places that humans cannot easily reach.
* The model and animations can remain simple enough for a solo developer.

Other playable animals could be considered later, but they are not required for the first prototype.

---

## First Environment

The first prototype will take place in a **small public park**.

The park should be intentionally small and manageable rather than a large open world.

Possible environmental elements include:

* Walking paths
* Grass
* Benches
* Trees
* Trash cans
* Food areas
* Human visitors
* Groups of NPC pigeons
* A ranger or other authority figure
* A clear escape location

The park should function as a compact stealth sandbox where the player can observe NPC routines and find different paths toward the objective.

---

## First Prototype Mission

The first playable mission is:

> **Steal food in the park without behaving suspiciously.**

The prototype should contain:

* One small park map
* One playable pigeon
* Several NPC pigeons
* One ranger or suspicious human
* One food objective
* One suspicion system
* One success condition
* One failure condition
* One escape point

The goal of the prototype is not to create the entire finished game immediately. Its purpose is to prove that pretending to be a normal animal is fun.

---

## Core Gameplay Loop

1. Enter an area as an animal.
2. Observe how the NPC animals behave.
3. Identify the mission objective.
4. Move toward the objective without appearing too purposeful.
5. Perform natural animal behaviors to blend in.
6. Watch nearby humans and their suspicion levels.
7. Back away, freeze, peck, wander, or join other animals when watched.
8. Complete the secret objective.
9. Reach the escape location.
10. Receive a score or mission result based on performance.

The player should constantly switch between **acting natural** and **making progress**.

---

## Natural Animal Behaviors

The pigeon should eventually be able to perform simple behaviors such as:

* Walking
* Turning
* Pecking at the ground
* Standing still
* Looking around
* Following other pigeons
* Approaching food
* Moving away from humans
* Flying or hopping short distances
* Joining a group of pigeons

These actions are not only animations. They are part of the stealth system.

For example, pecking while near food may lower suspicion, while sprinting directly toward an important item may increase suspicion.

---

## Suspicious Behaviors

Humans may become suspicious when the player:

* Moves directly toward important objects.
* Changes direction too perfectly.
* Runs without an obvious reason.
* Repeatedly jumps or flies.
* Stares at a human for too long.
* Separates from the other animals.
* Enters restricted areas.
* Interacts with objects in an unnatural way.
* Repeats the same behavior too often.
* Continues moving while being closely watched.

The game should avoid punishing every small mistake instantly. Suspicion should gradually build so the player has an opportunity to recover.

---

## Suspicion System

Each important human NPC should have a suspicion value.

A basic suspicion system could work like this:

* **Unaware:** The human ignores the animal.
* **Noticing:** The human briefly watches the animal.
* **Suspicious:** The human follows or investigates the animal.
* **Alerted:** The human realizes the animal is behaving unnaturally.
* **Chasing:** The human attempts to catch or remove the animal.

Suspicion should increase through unnatural behavior and decrease when the player:

* Stops moving.
* Walks away from the objective.
* Pecking or performs another natural action.
* Joins a group of NPC animals.
* Breaks the human’s line of sight.
* Waits until the human loses interest.

The system should be understandable through visual and audio feedback without requiring a complicated interface.

---

## NPC Animal Behavior

NPC animals are an important part of the gameplay because they show the player how a normal animal should act.

NPC pigeons should use simple randomized behavior states, such as:

* Idle
* Wander
* Peck
* Follow food
* Avoid humans
* Join a group
* Fly away when frightened
* Return after danger passes

They do not need advanced intelligence for the first prototype. Their main purpose is to make the park feel alive and give the player a behavioral pattern to imitate.

---

## Human and Ranger Behavior

Human NPCs should initially treat the player like a normal animal.

A ranger or suspicious human can:

* Walk along a patrol route.
* Look toward nearby animals.
* Investigate strange movement.
* Approach the player when suspicion increases.
* Chase the player after becoming fully alerted.
* Return to their normal route after losing interest.

The first ranger should remain simple enough to build with a state machine.

Suggested ranger states:

* Patrol
* Observe
* Investigate
* Chase
* Search
* Return to patrol

---

## Objectives

The first objective is stealing food, but future missions could use the same behavior-stealth system for different tasks.

Possible future objective types include:

* Steal an item.
* Retrieve food for another animal.
* Enter a restricted location.
* Distract a human.
* Deliver an object.
* Follow a person without being noticed.
* Cause a specific event.
* Free or help another animal.
* Learn information by observing humans.
* Escape an area after completing a mission.

These are expansion possibilities, not requirements for the first prototype.

---

## Failure Conditions

The player may fail when:

* The suspicion meter becomes completely full.
* A ranger catches the player.
* The player loses or destroys the mission item.
* The mission timer expires, if a timer is used.
* The player leaves the mission area without completing the objective.

The first prototype should use a simple failure condition: the ranger identifies and catches the pigeon.

---

## Success Conditions

The player succeeds by:

1. Reaching the food.
2. Taking or interacting with it.
3. Avoiding capture.
4. Reaching the escape point.

A mission result could later evaluate:

* Completion time
* Highest suspicion reached
* Number of humans alerted
* Amount of time spent blending in
* Optional objectives completed
* Whether the player was ever chased

---

## Camera and Controls

The game will use a simple **third-person or slightly elevated third-person camera**.

The camera should make it easy to:

* See the pigeon.
* Observe nearby NPC animals.
* Watch human sightlines.
* Locate mission objectives.
* Move through the environment without complicated controls.

The exact camera angle can be adjusted during prototyping based on which view makes the behavior-stealth mechanics easiest to understand.

Basic controls should include:

* Move forward
* Move backward
* Move left
* Move right
* Camera movement
* Natural behavior or peck action
* Short hop or flight action
* Interact or grab action

Controls should remain intentionally simple.

---

## Art Direction

The game will use a **cute, stylized, low-poly 3D art style**.

Visual goals:

* Simple blocky animal models
* Bright and readable environments
* Clear silhouettes
* Expressive animations
* Slightly exaggerated human reactions
* A playful indie-game appearance
* Models that are achievable for a solo developer

The project should not chase photorealism. The visual style should support humor, readability, and manageable development.

---

## Animation Direction

Animations can remain simple and slightly exaggerated.

Important first animations include:

* Pigeon idle
* Pigeon walk
* Pigeon peck
* Pigeon turn
* Pigeon hop
* Pigeon short flight
* Pigeon startled reaction
* Ranger walk
* Ranger observe
* Ranger chase

The animations should communicate behavior clearly, even if they are not highly detailed.

---

## Sound Direction

The sound design should help the player understand suspicion and danger.

Possible sounds include:

* Pigeon coos
* Wing flaps
* Footsteps
* Park ambience
* Human conversations
* A subtle sound when someone notices the player
* Increasing tension as suspicion rises
* A chase cue
* A success sound when the objective is completed

The audio should stay playful and should not make the game feel like a dark horror game.

---

## User Interface

The first version should have a minimal interface.

Possible UI elements:

* Current objective
* Suspicion meter
* Interaction prompt
* Mission success message
* Mission failure message
* Pause menu
* Restart button

The UI should not overwhelm the screen. Character animations, human reactions, and sound should communicate as much information as possible.

---

## Technology and Development Tools

### Game Engine

The game will be developed in **Godot 4**.

Godot will be used for:

* Scenes
* Player movement
* Camera controls
* NPC behavior
* Human AI
* Mission logic
* Suspicion system
* User interface
* Sound
* Exporting the finished game

### 3D Modeling

**Blender** will be used to create and edit:

* The pigeon
* Human characters
* Park props
* Trees
* Benches
* Trash cans
* Food objects
* Other environmental assets

Godot is the game engine. Blender is the main 3D modeling and animation program.

### Initial Platform

The intended first platform is **Windows PC**.

The project should be designed around keyboard and mouse controls first. Controller support and other platforms can be considered after the main gameplay is working.

---

## Development Approach

The project should be built in small stages.

### Stage 1: Basic 3D Setup

* Create a Godot 4 project.
* Create `Main.tscn`.
* Add a basic environment.
* Add lighting.
* Add a camera.
* Add a temporary pigeon player object.
* Make the pigeon move correctly.

### Stage 2: Pigeon Behaviors

* Add walking.
* Add turning.
* Add pecking.
* Add idle behavior.
* Add a short hop or flight.
* Connect animations to player actions.

### Stage 3: NPC Pigeons

* Add several NPC pigeons.
* Give them simple randomized states.
* Make them wander and peck.
* Make them react to humans or danger.

### Stage 4: Ranger

* Add a ranger.
* Give the ranger a patrol route.
* Add vision or detection.
* Add observe, investigate, and chase states.

### Stage 5: Suspicion System

* Detect unnatural player behavior.
* Increase and decrease suspicion.
* Add visible or audio feedback.
* Allow the player to recover by acting naturally.

### Stage 6: First Mission

* Add the food objective.
* Add an interaction system.
* Add an escape point.
* Add success and failure states.
* Add a restart option.

### Stage 7: Polish

* Replace temporary models.
* Improve animations.
* Add sound.
* Improve lighting and environment details.
* Balance suspicion.
* Fix bugs.
* Export a playable Windows build.

---

## Scope Rules

Because this is being developed as a solo project, the first version must remain small.

The first prototype should **not** begin with:

* A large open world
* Online multiplayer
* Many playable animals
* Complicated dialogue systems
* Dozens of missions
* Advanced procedural generation
* Realistic graphics
* Highly complicated combat
* A massive inventory system
* A full story campaign

The first goal is to build one polished and enjoyable park mission.

After the main behavior-stealth mechanic is proven, the project can expand.

---

## Multiplayer Position

Co-op, local play, or multiplayer has been discussed as something desirable for a game project, but **Don’t Look Human is currently being designed as a single-player prototype first**.

Multiplayer should not be added until the following systems work properly:

* Player movement
* Animal behaviors
* NPC animal behavior
* Human suspicion
* Objectives
* Mission success and failure

A future co-op version could allow multiple animals to work together, distract humans, or complete connected objectives, but that is not part of the first build.

---

## What Makes the Game Unique

The game’s main hook is that the player does not hide by disappearing.

The player is often standing in plain sight.

The challenge is convincing human NPCs that the player is only a normal animal.

Traditional stealth asks:

> “Can the enemy see you?”

Don’t Look Human asks:

> “Can the enemy see that you are thinking?”

This distinction is the identity of the game and should guide every major design decision.

---

## Final Conclusion

The final game I am making is **Don’t Look Human**, a low-poly 3D behavior-stealth game developed in Godot 4, with Blender used for models and animations.

The player begins as a pigeon in a small park filled with NPC pigeons and humans. The player must imitate natural animal behavior, manage human suspicion, secretly steal food, and escape without being identified as an unusually intelligent animal.

The first complete prototype will contain one park, one playable pigeon, several NPC pigeons, one ranger, one food-stealing objective, a suspicion system, and clear success and failure conditions.

The central rule of the entire project is:

> **Move like an animal, not like a gamer.**

The game’s defining idea is not simply playing as an animal. It is hiding the fact that a human mind is controlling the animal.

## Instructions for an AI Development Assistant

You are helping develop a solo indie game called **Don’t Look Human**.

Always follow these rules when helping with the project:

1. Use Godot 4-compatible instructions and code.
2. Assume the first target platform is Windows PC.
3. Use Blender for original 3D models and animations.
4. Keep solutions beginner-friendly and explain exactly where files, nodes, scripts, and settings belong.
5. Provide complete code that can be copied and pasted.
6. Do not skip setup steps.
7. Do not assume nodes, input actions, folders, or scripts already exist.
8. Clearly state whether each instruction belongs in Godot, Blender, the Inspector, the Scene tree, the FileSystem panel, Project Settings, or a script.
9. Build one system at a time and test it before adding another.
10. Keep the first prototype limited to one park, one pigeon, NPC pigeons, one ranger, one food objective, one escape point, and one suspicion system.
11. Preserve the central behavior-stealth mechanic.
12. Avoid turning the game into a normal combat game, endless survival game, or generic animal simulator.
13. Remember that the core fantasy is acting like a normal animal while secretly completing intelligent objectives.
14. When giving Godot instructions, use terminology and features compatible with Godot 4.7 whenever possible.
15. Prioritize a working prototype over advanced graphics or unnecessary features.
